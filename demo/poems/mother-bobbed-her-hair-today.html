Mother Bobbed Her Hair Today

D.H. Elton

Mother Bobbed Her Hair Today These verses were written to my daughter, Viva, then in Bangor, Maine, U.S.A., February 12th, 1925 — the day of the decapitation. Mother bobbed her hair today, Yes, she did, and daughter, say Don’t let on, but really she Doesn’t look too bad to me. Sort of curious like, by heck, Where the clippers trimmed her neck, Don’t know yet just what to say: Mother bobbed her hair today. She came in the parlour — there — Stood beside my easy chair, Sort o’ stroked me on the chin, Then she gently started in, “Dearie, do you like my hair?” I looked ‘round with solemn stare; Couldn’t tell if I’d been shot, Whether it was bobbed or not. Then she laughed, I looked again; Sure enough, it was quite plain. Tresses gone, I changed my mind — Dinky, kinky curls behind. Made out I was sore and vexed, Angry, hurt, and much perplexed. But, ‘twas done, what could I say: Mother bobbed her hair today. Why do women bob their hair? Don’t ask me, for I declare I don’t know, but here’s some guesses, Why they bob their pretty tresses; Maybe it’s because they’re fearful They’ll be called Old Rip Van Winkle, Covered wagon, Horse and Mane, Or some Prehistoric Jane. Then they come to you, confessing That it saves a lot of dressing; Night and morning, so, ‘tis said All they do is “shake their head.” •D.H. Elton•
