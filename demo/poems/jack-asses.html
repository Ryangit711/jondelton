“Jack-asses”

D.H. Elton

Two fool "Jack-asses", one summer's day, Tied together with a rope they say. Were placed in a field where the hay was piled, And left to feast, all unbeguiled. One said to the other: You come my way, And let me eat some new-mown hay." But the other replied: "Get off your beat-- You come with me, so I can eat." They couldn't agree or get together-- They simply tested, and strained their tether-- They pawed up the earth, and mulled the soil, With no reward for their useless toil. And, so they pulled, on either hand, And struggled, and sweat, to beat the band. They tugged, and lugged, and milled away, But got no nearer the new-mown hay. They got "Nowhere" by striving so, But landed up in "status quo," When face to face, near the end of day, They drooped their heads, and seemed to say: We're just a couple of Jack-ass fools, A couple of thick-skinned, stubborn mules-- Our necks are sore, we're hungry besides, While there's plenty to eat if we'd just be wise. Said number one: "You come with me, Why pull apart? Why not agree? Let's eat together as we go along, And we'll both be filled 'ere the day is done." So, they went together, and ate their hay, The rope was kind, when they went one way-- They browsed contented from pile to pile, With a cheerful bray, and a happy smile. A lesson here, for human folks, Who act like thick skinned stubborn mokes. In times of doubt, distress, despair, Let's pull together and get our share. And Nations, too--forget your pride-- Don't strain the bonds by which we are tied: A moral from the "Jack-ass" learn-- And pull together at every turn.
