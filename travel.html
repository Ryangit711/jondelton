Travel Adventures

Jon and Ron’s most excellent Malaysia / Borneo / Thailand adventure

Just a bit of a pre-amble. My wife Mary’s brother Ron Running has caught the travel bug and convinced me to go on a Friendly Planet tour of Kuala Lumpur, Borneo, and Thailand. We travelled with Drew and Joanne Cahoon our mutual friends, Bill and Paula Glade, Ron’s friends from previous Friendly Planet trips and two other couples we didn’t know before, Andrew and Lydia, and Sheldon and Mary, both from New York.

The Friendly Planet part of the trip ends after Bangkok when we bid goodbye to the other travellers and Ron, myself & the Cahoons went on to Chiang Mai, Thailand for the final stage of our trip.

This journal is mainly for myself and my travelling companions to remind us of the fabulous time we had and then for any others who might find it interesting. It was a great trip and we made some great memories. I hope you enjoy this small insight into these amazing areas of the world and the fun we had there.

Part One — Kuala Lumpur

(Fun just to say it!)

Ron and I met up with Drew and Joanne in the LAX airport. From there we flew 14 hours to Taipei, Taiwan where we met up with other members of our Friendly Planet tour group and continued on to Kuala Lumpur.

Our first night Ron arranged for us to have dinner with an old colleague from when Ron worked in Corona, California as a city planner. His friend who went by Mark while he lived in the US, but whose real name is Mohammed, and his son Adam treated us to a wonderful dinner at a Malay restaurant near our hotel. Very nice and very generous of him. He kept calling Ron “boss”.

His son Adam, a nice young man, is going to Germany to study engineering and is an Uber driver to make money in the mean time. The Malaysian food was wonderful but apparently not that popular here as we really had to search to find the restaurant. There is a lot of Thai and Japanese restaurants, not to mention McDonalds, KFC, Pizza Hut etc. Just like home.

We stayed at the Pullman hotel which is a new one for me but it must be a good one as it has a fabulous breakfast buffet and the King of one of the states that make up the Malaysia federation was staying there also on some kind of a state visit. Consequently there was a lot of police staying in the hotel too. It was interesting seeing a lot of young women motorcycle police all wearing their Muslim head scarves.

Malaysia is about 60% Muslim though there are lots of Chinese and Indians living here too. It is a very multi racial society that seems to respect the minorities rights very well. They have amazing buildings and architecture and even bigger ambitions for the future with tremendous building plans. Quite amazing!! As we drove to the airport we saw literally dozens of high rise apartments being built. The building going on here is incredible.

First morning we took a tour of the city and visited a number of museums and interesting buildings. KL has an amazing history from pre colonial times to being a British colony to getting their independence after the Japanese occupation when the second world war ended. They just didn’t invite the British back in the same way, though there was some kind of relationship until about 1960 when with the help of the British they defeated a Communist rebellion at which time the British left for good.

They do have a King as a figure head monarch and we visited the Royal Palace. In a very democratic way they decided to rotate the Kingship in 5 year increments between the 9 royal families or Sultans that make up the states of the federation. So if you are a local Sultan, you get to be King every 45 years with only one ever repeating. Interestingly Singapore was one of the Malaysian states but decided to cede from the union and go it on their own. One of the group suggested it was like what Texas would like to do.

We visited a museum that had a room that showed Kuala Lumpur’s vision of the future. Wow, these people are really ambitious!! Huge buildings, amazing architecture, parks and living spaces including the tallest building in the world. (They seem to have a thing about tall buildings.) I guess we shall see if it comes to fruition.

In the afternoon we went on a tour of the Petronas twin towers which are the tallest in the world with Andrew and Lydia. Andrew’s idea and a good one. Not much to say about the towers other than “really big, with a great view of the area!” At the towers we met Chawna Volken and her sister Camette and did the tour together. Quite a coincidence that we were in Malaysia at the same time.

Two years ago when they first planned the trip they would very likely have been on the Malaysian airplane that famously disappeared without a trace. Sadly 20 employees for Camette’s husbands company were on that flight and their loss caused the company to abandon the project they were working on there. Very sad and amazing to think that Chawna might have been on that plane.

We then walked back to our hotel through an underground mall. Really quite amazing. The weather here is very warm and humid so it was nice that we didn’t have to hike it above ground. That night our group through Friendly Planet, had a really nice dinner and cultural show at a large buffet style restaurant, very enjoyable. Lots of local singing and dancing and great food. Doesn’t look like we are going to lose any weight on this trip.

Part Two — Borneo

After a great Pullman Hotel breakfast we packed up and headed to the airport for our trip to the city of Kuching in Borneo. The leg room on the flight was non existent, obviously created for smaller Asian bodies, but the flight was fairly short and we all survived.

We were met at the airport by Matthew our Friendly Planet rep. It is always nice to have a friendly face meet you in a strange city. After an hours drive through the Borneo country side we arrived at the hotel in Kuching. We stayed at the Hilton which is very nice and centrally located. It is as warm and humid here as it was on mainland Malaysia which is to say, ‘very’. I suppose that you eventually get used to being wet and sweaty all the time. Not so Amigo! The humidity hits you like a wall.

Kuching is the capital of the state of Sarawak, here in Borneo, and is a very tidy multi racial city similar to KL only lots smaller. Also apparently, though you see a lot of Muslim head scarves here, the religious majority is Christian. Lots of Chinese, and not as many Indians. I am so taken by the diversity amongst the people and how they seem to get along so well.

After a shower, rest and short shopping trip in search of rain ponchos, we had dinner at a local tribal food cafe across from the hotel. Interesting. Drew scoped out the local massage scene and some of us ended the evening with massages.

Next morning after another wonderful breakfast buffet we headed off for our first Nature adventure! Borneo has a lot of unique animals and we were off by bus and boat to visit a National park to see some of them, mostly snakes and monkeys, though we did see a couple of wild boars though they didn’t seem too wild. Our guide for the day was Hussein who is from a small tribal village quite some distance away. His English was not the best but he was very knowledgeable and interesting.

When we arrived at the park it was low tide and we had to wade in from the boat to a beautiful beach with some amazing rock formations. From there we made our way to the Park’s welcome center from where we went on short hikes to see the animals. We saw a small Paradise snake that was eating a lizard. We also saw a lot of monkeys up in the trees. There are three main kinds of monkeys here: Proboscis (they have the big bulbous noses), Silver Leaf, and Long tail Macaque, the last one being the mischievous ones that will steal the food right off your plate, or out of your hut, both of which we witnessed. Cheeky little devils.

After one hike we had a nice lunch where we witnessed two Macaques relieve a nice lady of the chicken off her plate. She was not pleased! Just as we were getting ready to depart a thunder storm arrived with big wind and rain that came down in torrents. The boat ride back was beautiful and uneventful. That night some of us went to the Top Spot restaurant made famous by a visit by Anthony Bourdain.

The next day after another wonderful breakfast we headed off by bus half way across the country to a wilderness resort. We spent about 6 hours on the bus with a few interesting stops along the way in small towns and their markets. The first stop was at an Orangutan Rehab center. Very interesting. There is a group of around 27 Orangutans there, but it is an 800 acre park so you want to get there at feeding time, which we did.

We also had a couple of rest stops in small villages along the way that were interesting. In one there was a market that was quite busy and fun to browse. I really enjoy people watching here. The people seem so hard working and happy. Fun to watch them go through their every day lives.

As we drove through the countryside mostly on a bumpy two lane highway we got to see a lot of the rural life. They grow lots of rice here and we saw small fields almost everywhere. We also saw a lot of Palm Oil trees and rubber trees which are both major industries. They also grow a lot of pepper corn which is becoming a major export and of course Papaya and bananas.

We arrived at a huge lake created by a large hydro dam. There we transferred with all our belongings onto a small boat with a bunch of Dutch people to make our 25 minute boat ride to the Batang resort which is really out in the wild, though the resort was very nice with all the comforts of home. Kind of like some of the ones we stayed at in Kenya. Old British feel with lots of dark wood. Must have cost a fortune to build it out here in really the middle of nowhere.

Part Three — More Borneo

The Batang resort was very nice. Great pool, good food, interesting companions and an amazing environment. Jungle everywhere!

This morning we headed off by long boat to visit a long house in a native village. A long house is just as it sounds, a very long narrow building with a lot of families living together with their own personal areas and a long common area adjoining that they share. Quite an interesting arrangement. The children were playing together in the common areas, most of them cousins I was told. A couple of them had iPad like devices they were playing on, so not so different from our own kids in that way. There is a chief who arbitrates minor disputes and sends more serious ones on to the district courts.

Their only mode of transportation seems to be these long boats which makes sense as to go anywhere from the long house you have to go by boat. They showed us their homes, danced for us and with us and then fed us a traditional native lunch. It was very good. I guess everybody in the world eats chicken. There were some very interesting good native veggies and a very happy dog who got my chicken. Seems like there are dogs everywhere here.

We then presented them with the gifts we had brought, mostly treats, pencils and other fun trinkets. The children sat quietly in a circle while some of the women distributed the gifts from us and the Dutch. It was interesting that there was no squabbling amongst the children at all during the process. They all seemed happy to share and afterwards came and thanked us individually. That was nice.

Then after the requisite shopping opportunity, I bought a small blow gun with 4 darts, we took our long boats back to our resort for a welcome swim in the 90 degree pool. Some of us then went on a nature walk up the hill from our lodge that went through the jungle past the grave site of a famous warrior to a suspension bridge that took us up into the canopy of the jungle. It was quite neat with a lovely view to the lake.

We also learned that across the lake is Indonesia with whom, some time back, they had a conflict. Our guide told us about the local trees and herbs and what they are used for. The jungle is quite the medicine cabinet. It was a fun hike but we were all soaked with sweat by the end and it was time for another swim.

It really is quite amazing that we live in a time and circumstance that allow us to do so many things and see so much of the world in relative comfort. I am reminded of this every time we have a rest stop and get off our air conditioned bus to be met by a wall of heat and humidity.

Part Four — Bangkok

We arrived in Bangkok after a long day of travel starting in the remote jungle resort of Batang. We boated from the resort to the bus, which then took us on an almost 4 hour bus ride to Kuching where we just barely arrived in time to catch our plane to KL where after a 3 hour layover we board the plane to Bangkok where we arrived at our hotel at about 11:30 PM. Our hotel is very nice and named the Royal Orchid, a Sheraton Hotel right on a major river which is very busy with huge barges and ferries scurrying around non stop.

In Thailand they really loved their King. It is an interesting time in Thailand as their beloved King, of just over 70 years, King Rama the 9th, died a few days before we arrived and the whole country is in official mourning. It seems that he was a very responsible and caring king who, it seems, was pretty universally beloved. Even those who didn’t like the idea of a monarchy seemed to begrudgingly agree that he had been a good King. He had been King since he was 18 years old when his older brother King Rama 8, who was mysteriously shot to death in his sleep and Rama the 9th was made the new King that night and served as king for 70 years the longest reigning monarch in the world.

Next morning we met our guide Matthew at 8:30 AM for a tour of two temples and an interesting house. Because of the King’s death we won’t be able to visit the royal palace as it is closed to the public this week and everyone is supposed to wear black. Ron and I bought black shirts at the airport. Apparently a black market in black clothes sprang up and there was some concern over price gouging.

The first temple featured the Golden Buddha. It is huge and apparently solid 14 Carat gold. Story is that during a war many years ago residents of the village where it was found covered it with cement during a period of unrest so that if they were over run the enemy wouldn’t realize how valuable it was. That worked but apparently everyone who knew about it must have been killed because it wasn’t until a few hundred years later, when one of the Kings was moving it to another location, that it was dropped because it was so heavy and some of the concrete broke off, to reveal the amazing golden Buddha.

The second temple was the one with the large and famous Reclining Buddha. It is an amazing piece of work over 47 meters long, not solid gold, but covered in gold. Our third stop was the Jim Thompson estate. He was an American that fell in love with Thailand during the Second World War and moved there shortly afterwards. He was an architect and designer and fell in love with the Thai silk makers. He broke through when he supplied most of the costumes and silk for the movie The King And I with Yul Brenner.

He disappeared without a trace while he was on holidays in Kuala Lumpur and as he had no immediate family, his house, company and antiques became part of a foundation. You see Jim Thompson stores in most the malls and shopping areas. Very expensive! It was a fun visit.

After we got back to the hotel while others went to rest in their rooms, Ron and I went out to shop at the antique stores in an adjacent mall. Ron has become quite the shopper. We got back from that shopping just in time to head out with the group to the night market. To get there you take the free hotel shuttle boat to another landing where you catch the free boat to the market. At the night market Joanne and I did the Fish Doctor experience where you put your feet into an aquarium full of tiny little fish that nibble at your feet and legs. You can’t explain it, you have to experience it.

Next day we got up early to go see the floating market and what is called the train market which we went to first. The train market is right in the middle of a small town, about 45 minutes from Bangkok and is a market right on the train tracks. When the train comes through all the vendors pull their awnings and tables back from the rails to let the train pass. The train was moving quite slowly but it was fun to see.

From there we went to catch a boat to take us to the floating market. It was great fun on a turbo charged long boat through some very interesting neighbourhoods on the way to the market. Lots of coconut, banana, and Papaya trees seemingly everywhere. The market was not at all like I expected, but great fun. This market had all the touristy stuff, with great bargaining and getting to know the local merchants in a rather interesting setting on and around the river. All the prices were at least 2 or 3 times higher than they would settle for. It is a bit of an art to get a good deal and still stay on friendly terms.

The tiny coconut pancakes Matthew recommended were wonderful and lots of amazing sights, sounds and smells. It was a fun morning. There was one older wizened woman who seemed to particularly take a liking to me. She was a sales person “at large” which meant she could follow you anywhere, selling small containers of Tiger Balm completely convinced that I needed a lot of it and proceeded to anoint various parts of my body with it. After some negotiation I ended up with the family sized Tiger Balm. A fun little lady that gives a great temple massage.

Part Five — Chiang Mai

We caught a Bangkok Air flight, a really interesting airline. They have their own lounge with internet, newspapers and snacks even for economy class customers. The employees are very polite and well trained and really seem to go the extra mile. There was even a meal on the slightly more than one hour flight. Not bad leg room either! Amazing in today’s world of airlines and travel.

We arrived to be greeted by our Friendly Planet rep who took us to our hotel resort called Siripanna. What an interesting place. It has its own rice paddies where the guests can plant rice.

First morning we headed off with our Friendly Planet guide to go to an Elephant refuge and then up in the hills to see a long neck tribe village. The Elephant place was interesting. We went to a place where the Elephants played soccer, threw darts at balloons, drew paintings, and gave rides. There were a lot of families there and everyone seemed to have a great time, even the Elephants… hopefully. We had our pictures taken while we fed them bananas and sugar cane, watched them bathe in the river and their paintings were surprisingly good.

From there we went to a village area where there were a number of different native tribes, the most noticeable of which is the Long Throated people. Though here were 4 other tribes too, including the ‘long ear lobe’ tribe. The ‘long neckers’ put rings around their necks of the girls who are born on Thursday or Friday to lengthen their necks. I suppose they feel it makes them more beautiful. Kind of reminds me of the binding of the feet that they used to do in China.

Friday and Saturday we were with Nan, the Cahoon’s exchange student and her family. Nan is taking Political Science and wants to become an Ambassador, and she just could make it! Her family lives in Chiang Mai so we had our own tour guides which was fun.

We started by driving quite a ways up a mountain to a Buddhist temple that was quite amazing. There were a lot of visitors from devout Buddhists to tourists like us. We then went further up the mountain to the King’s palace. A secondary palace that they go to when it gets too hot in Bangkok. Chiang Mai is higher up and definitely cooler.

From there we came down the mountain and went to lunch at a fun organic restaurant started by 3 guys who grow all the food for their restaurants in their own green houses. Wonderful salads!! After lunch Air got us the opportunity to visit the Bernina factory where she worked until just recently. It was really interesting. You can imagine how complicated these modern sewing machines are, and they not only assemble them, they also manufacture most of the parts here. Quite amazing!!

From there we went to the big Buddhist temple in Chiang Mai. Beautiful! They have so many beautiful temples. On the top of a huge Pagoda near the temple is an alcove which houses the Emerald Buddha a gift from the King to the area.

After dinner Air took us down to the night market. One very busy street with practically everything Thai that you could ever want. As the trip is winding down we are spending our last few Baht for the last few souvenirs we need. It was interesting to see so many people eating, shopping, and enjoying life. A nice ending to our last night in Thailand.

The last night we had a fabulous dinner at Nan’s home. Manoch, her father, had been working all day on our special Thai dinner, and what a wonderful dinner it was!! 8 or more courses and every one wonderful! We tried a number of fruits we had never tried before that were really delicious! Dessert was sticky rice with sweet coconut milk and lots of Mango. Boy was that good. They were so gracious to us it really was a special night.

Next morning Air came to the hotel to see us off. She is so nice and so genuinely happy to be with us. She and her family really made the Chiang Mai leg of our trip wonderful.

I hope you enjoyed this small insight into these amazing areas of the world and the fun we had there. It was a great trip and we made some great memories.
