# Jon and Ron&#8217;s most excellent Malaysia  / Borneo / Thailand Adventure.

Jon and Ron @ Taipei Airport.

Just a bit of a pre-amble.  My wife Mary’s brother Ron Running has caught the travel bug and convinced me to go on a Friendly Planet tour of Kuala Lumpur, Borneo, and Thailand.  We travelled with Drew and Joanne Cahoon our mutual friends, Bill and Paula Glade, Ron’s friends from previous Friendly Planet trips and two other couples we didn’t know before, Andrew and Lydia, and Sheldon and Mary, both from New York.

The Friendly Planet part of the trip ends after Bangkok when we bid goodbye to the other travellers and Ron, myself and the Cahoons went on to Chiang Mai, Thailand for the final stage of our trip.

This journal is mainly for myself and my travelling companions to remind us of the fabulous time we had and then for any others who might find it interesting.
  
It was a great trip and we made some great memories.
  
I hope you enjoy this small insight into these amazing areas of the world and the fun we had there.

Part One — Kuala Lumpur.
  
(Fun just to say it!)

       
Ron and I met up with Drew and Joanne in the LAX airport. From there we flew 14 hours to Taipei, Taiwan where we met up with other members of our friendly planet tour group and continued on to Kuala Lumpur.
  
Our first night Ron arranged for us to have dinner with an old colleague from when Ron worked in Corona, California as a city planner.
  
His friend who went by Mark while he lived in the US, but whose real name is Mohammed, and his son Adam treated us to a wonderful dinner at a Malay restaurant near our hotel.
  
Very nice and very generous of him.
  
He kept calling Ron "boss".
  
His son Adam, a nice young man, is going to Germany to study engineering and is an Uber driver to make money In the mean time.  The Malaysian food was wonderful but apparently not that popular here as we really had to search to find the restaurant.

Just like home.
   

        
We stayed at the Pullman hotel which is a new one for me but it must be a good one as it has a fabulous breakfast buffet and the King of one of the states that make up the Malaysia federation was staying there also on some kind of a state visit. Consequently there was a lot of police staying in the hotel too.
  
It was interesting seeing a lot of young women motorcycle police all wearing their Muslim head scarves.
  
Malaysia is about 60% Muslim though there are lots of Chinese and Indians living here too. It is a very multi racial society that seems to respect the minorities rights very well. They have amazing buildings and architecture and even bigger ambitions for the future with tremendous building plans.
  
Quite amazing!!
  
As we drove to the airport we saw literally dozens of high rise apartments being built. The building going on here is incredible.

Joanne photo bombing Andrew and Lydia in front of the King's Palace in KL.

      
First morning we took a tour of the city and visited a number of museums and interesting buildings. KL has an amazing history from pre colonial times to being a British colony to getting their independence after the Japanese occupation when the second world war ended.
  
They just didn’t invite the British back in the same way, though there was some kind of relationship until about 1960 when with the help of the British they defeated a Communist rebellion at which time the British left for good.
  
The Japanese left after Atomic bombs were dropped on Hiroshima and Nagasaki.

     
They do have a King as a figure head monarch and we visited the Royal Palace.
  
In a very democratic way they decided to rotate the Kingship in 5 year increments between the 9 royal families or Sultans that make up the states of the federation. So if you are a local Sultan, you get to be King every 45 years with only one ever repeating.
  
With the regular changing of the King, the people don’t seem to have the relationship with their monarch that they have in Thailand which we learned more later in our trip. Interestingly Singapore was one of the Malaysian states but decided to cede from the union and go it on their own.
  
One of the group suggested it was like what Texas would like to do.
           

Ron and I in front of the Petronas Towers.

We visited a museum that had a room that showed Kuala Lumpur’s vision of the future.
  
Wow, these people are really ambitious!!
  
Huge buildings, amazing architecture, parks and living spaces including the tallest building in the world.
  
(They seem to have a thing about tall buildings.)
  
I guess we shall see if it comes to fruition.

In the afternoon we went on a tour of the Petronas twin towers which are the tallest in the world with Andrew and Lydia. Andrew’s idea and a good one. Not much to say about the towers other than "really big, with a great view of the area!"
  
At the towers we met Chawna Volken and her sister Camette and did the tour together. Quite a coincidence that we were in Malaysia at the same time. It was fun to spend some time with them and to hear the story about their trip.
  
Two years ago when they first planned the trip they would very likely have been on the Malaysian airplane that famously disappeared without a trace.
  
Sadly 20 employees for Camette's husbands company were on that flight and their loss caused the company to abandon the project they were working on there.
  
Very sad and amazing to think that Chawna might have been on that plane.

      
We then walked back to our hotel through an underground mall. Really quite amazing.
  
The weather here is very warm and humid so it was nice that we didn't have to hike it above ground. So far lot of warm humidity and over cast, but no rain.

Doesn't look like we are going to lose any weight on this trip. Breakfasts in the hotels have also been sensational.
  
Slept close to 6 hours that night which is good. Ron doesn't seem to snore which is good too. Not sure about me.

Kuala Lumpur was a real eye-opener.

After a great Pullman Hotel breakfast we packed up and headed to the airport for our trip to the city of Kuching in Borneo.
  
(I do tend to carry on about the breakfasts don’t I.)
  
The leg room on the flight was non existent, obviously created for smaller Asian bodies, but the flight was fairly short and we all survived.

Never used the Tennis Court or Pool.

       
We were met at the airport by Matthew our Friendly Planet rep.
  
It is always nice to have a friendly face meet you in a strange city.
  
After an hours drive through the Borneo country side, didn't
  
seem too wild so far, we arrived at the hotel in Kuching. We are stayed at the Hilton which is very nice and centrally located. It is as warm and humid here as it was on mainland Malaysia which is to say, &#8216;very'. I suppose that you eventually get used to being wet and sweaty all the time. When you are in your air conditioned hotel looking out at a grey overcast day you assume it is cooler outside than in.
  
Not so Amigo!

Boy tennis would be hard work in this humidity.

       
Kuching, is the capital of the state of Sarawak, here in Borneo, and is a very tidy multi racial city similar to KL only lots smaller.
  
Also apparently, though you see a lot of Muslim head scarves here, the religious majority is Christian.
  
Lots of Chinese, and not as many Indians.
  
I am so taken by the diversity amongst the people and how they seem to get along so well.

      
After a shower rest and short shopping trip in search of rain ponchos, we had dinner at a local tribal food cafe across from the hotel.
  
Interesting.
  
We had the opportunity to do karaoke there but as our Malay is still pretty weak we passed, thought Bill, Ron's surgeon friend from Minnesota does surprise our waiters on occasion by ordering in Malay.
  
Clever fellow. Drew scoped out the local massage scene and some of us ended the evening with massages.

A monkey in the National Park we visited.

       
Next morning after another wonderful breakfast buffet, I could get used to my own custom made omelette every day, we headed off for our first Nature adventure!
  
Borneo has a lot of unique animals and we were off by bus and boat to visit a National park to see some of them, mostly snakes and monkeys, though we did see a couple of wild boars though they didn't seem too wild. Our guide for the day was Hussein who is from a small tribal village quite some distance away.
  
His English was not the best but he was very knowledgable and interesting.
  
He is Muslim, as his name would suggest, but he talked a lot about his tribe and how they are a small minority group. Here on Borneo there are more native tribal groups it seems.

Rock formation at low tide.

     
When we arrived at the park it was low tide and we had to wade in from the boat to a beautiful beach with some amazing rock formations. From there we made our way to the Park’s welcome center from where we went on short hikes to see the animals. We saw a small Paradise snake that was eating a lizard.
  
We also saw a lot of monkeys up in the trees.
  
There are three main kinds of monkeys here.
  
Proboscis, they have the big bulbous noses, Silver Leaf, and Long tail Macaque, the last one being the mischievous ones that will steal the food right off your plate, or out of your hut, both of which we witnessed.
  
Cheeky little devils.
  
After one hike we had a nice lunch where we witnessed two Macaques relieve a nice lady of the chicken off her plate.
  
She was not pleased!
  
Just as we were getting ready to depart a thunder storm arrived with big wind and rain that came down in torrents. Luckily we had just gotten back from our hike and

did not get caught in it though it did delay our departure by 30 minutes or so.
  
The boat ride back was beautiful and uneventful.      That night some of us went to the Top Spot restaurant made famous by a visit by Anthony Bourdaine.  Really a food fair type of thing with a number of mostly seafood restaurants with a common eating area. We were told to go to #25 which was by far the most and had prawns, chicken, veggies and tofu.  It was very tasty.  Good catch Anthony.

  
The next day after another wonderful breakfast and headed off by bus half way across the country to a wilderness resort.
  
Did I mention Borneo is the 3rd largest island in the world!!
  
We spent about 6 hours on the bus with a few interesting stops along the way in small towns and their markets.
   

The first stop was at an Orangutang Rehab center.
  
Very interesting.
  
There is a group of around 27 Orangutans there, but it is an 800 acre park so you want to get there at feeding time, which we did, but even that doesn't guarantee anything.
  
Richie, the alpha male didn't show up today but two of his girlfriends, one of which was his grand daughter, came with their two most recent off spring.
  
There can only be one Alpha male and our guide indicated that Richie was getting challenged by some of his sons for that position in the clan. It seems to me that the gene pool is fairly small in this clan.
  
It was fun to see them in there natural habitat and I got some good pictures.

    
We also had a couple of rest stops in small villages along the way that were interesting.
  
In one there was a market that was quite busy and fun to browse.
  
At another we had a very nice lunch.
  
I really enjoy people watching here. The people seem so hard working and happy. Fun to watch them go through their every day lives.

     
As we drove through the countryside mostly on a bumpy two lane highway we got to see a lot of the rural life.
  
They grow lots of rice here and we saw small fields almost everywhere.
  
We also saw a lot of Palm Oil trees and rubber trees which are both major industries. They also grow a lot of pepper corn which is becoming a major export and of course Papaya and bananas. Fun to see the people working in their fields as we whizzed by in our air conditioned bus.
  
The drivers seem quite careful and it is not like driving in Bali or Cairo where you felt you might die at any second.

Drew chatting up the Dutch.

    
We arrived at a huge lake created by a large hydro dam. There we transferred with all our belongings onto a small boat with a bunch of Dutch people to make our 25 minute boat ride to the Batang resort which is really out in the wild, though the resort was very nice with all the comforts of home. Kind of like some of the ones we stayed at in Kenya.
  
Old British feel with lots of dark wood.
  
Must have cost a fortune to build it out here in really the middle of nowhere.

Part Three. More Borneo.
  
(Written mostly on the bus driving from the Batang Resort back to Kuching where we will catch a plane to Kuala Lumpur where we will catch a plane to Bangkok.

The Batang resort was very nice. Great pool, good food, interesting companions and an amazing environment.
  
Jungle everywhere!

(Seems like you can’t get away from cel phone towers now a days.)

      
This morning we headed off by long boat to visit a long house in a native village.
  
A long house is just as it sounds, a very long narrow building with a lot of families living together with their own personal areas and a long common area adjoining that they share. Quite an interesting arrangement that is mimicked by the rooms at our resort. The children were playing together in the common areas, most of them cousins I was told. A couple of them had iPad like devices they were playing on, so not so different from our own kids in that way. There is a chief who arbitrates minor disputes and sends more serious ones on to the district courts. There is lots of jungle around the long house and if anyone wants to clear land and farm it they can do that and claim the land for their own. Interesting system.
  

Their only mode of transportation seems to be these long boats which makes sense as to go anywhere from the long house you have to go by boat.

     
They showed us their homes, danced for us and with us and then fed us a traditional native lunch.
  
It was very good.
  
I guess everybody in the world eats chicken.
  
There were some very interesting good native veggies and a very happy dog who got my chicken. Seems like there are dogs everywhere here.

      
We then presented them with the gifts we had brought, mostly treats, pencils and other fun trinkets. The children sat quietly in a circle while some of the women distributed the gifts from us and the Dutch.
 It was interesting that there was no squabbling amongst the children at all during the process.
  
They all seemed happy to share and afterwards came and thanked us individually.
  
That was nice.

      
Then after the requisite shopping opportunity, I bought a small blow gun with 4 darts, we took our long boats back to our resort for a welcome swim in the 90 degree pool.
  
Not that refreshing but nice none the less.
  
Some of us then went on a nature walk up the hill from our lodge that went through the jungle past the grave site of a famous warrior to a suspension bridge that took us up into the canopy of the jungle.
  
It was quite neat with a lovely view to the lake.
  
 

We also learned that across the lake is Indonesia with whom, some time back, they had a conflict.
  
I guess Suharto the leader of Indonesia had some expansionist ideas, but the Malaysians fought them off.
  
Our guide told us about the local trees and herbs and what they are used for.
  
The jungle is quite the medicine cabinet.
  
It was a fun hike but we were all soaked with sweat by the end and it was time for another swim.

     
The air conditioning in our rooms worked great and as I was having a wonderful shower this morning I was thinking about the kind of infrastructure that they had to build out here in the remotest jungles of Borneo so we with our Western sensibilities can visit in relative comfort!
  
And we tourists are appreciative, but heaven help them if the Wifi doesn't work quite right, or the Creme Caramel in the buffet isn't quite as it should be!
  
(By the way, the Creme Caramel was excellent last night.).

I am reminded of this every time we have a rest stop and get off our air conditioned bus to be met by a wall of heat and humidity. By the way, at each rest stop we have the opportunity to buy stuff from the local shops.
  
It has been really pleasant dealing with the local shop keepers who try to be helpful but are not aggressive at all which is nice.

Part Four. 
  
Bangkok.

      
We arrived in Bangkok after a long day of travel starting in the remote jungle resort of Batang.
  
We boated from the resort to the bus, which then took us on an almost 4 hour bus ride to Kutching where we just barely arrived in time to catch our plane to KL where after a 3 hour layover we board the plane to Bangkok where we arrived at our hotel at about 11:30 PM.
  
Our hotel is very nice and named the Royal Orchid, a Sheraton Hotel right on a major river which is very busy with huge barges and ferries scurrying around non stop. We got to sleep sometime after 1 AM.
  
Sleep has not been coming easily for either Ron or I.

In Thailand they really loved their King.

It is an interesting time in Thailand as their beloved King, of just over 70 years, King Rama the 9th. died a few days before we arrived and the whole country is in official mourning.
  
It seems that he was a very responsible and caring king who, it seems, was pretty universally beloved.
  
Even those who didn't like the idea of a monarchy seemed to begrudgingly agree that he had been a good King.
  
If you had to have a King. He had been King since he was 18 years old when his older brother King Rama 8, who was mysteriously shot to death in his sleep and Rama the 9th was made the new King that night and served as king for 70 years the longest reigning monarch in the world. 
       

       
Interestingly there is a law in Thailand that no one is allowed to talk about the royal family in negative terms.
  
The official version of Rama 8th death is "fire arms accident". Hummmmm.
  
A bit of controversy right now is that the son of Rama 9 who has been a bit of a playboy throughout his

The airport with Bill, Paula, Matthew & the king.

life returned to Thailand the day before his fathers death, but decided not to become the new King immediately as his father did, but to wait a while with a 96 year old regent acting as King until he is ready.
  
Apparently there can never be a moment without someone being at least acting as the king.
  
The younger sister is more popular than he, as she stuck around and was doing Royal family type stuff while he was off in Europe most of the time, but there is no precedent for a daughter to become King.
  
Unless, maybe there is another &#8216;fire arms accident maybe?.

Ron and the Gold Buddha.

      
Next morning we met out guide Matthew at 8:30 AM for a tour of two temples and an interesting house. 
  
Because of the Kings death we won’t be able to visit the royal palace as it is closed to the public this week and everyone is supposed to wear black.
  
Ron and I bought black shirts at the airport. Apparently a black market in black clothes sprang up and there was some concern over price gouging.
  
So off we went on our first tour that had an extra temple in place of the royal palace. The first temple featured the Golden Buddha.
  
It is huge and apparently solid 14 Carat gold.
  
Story is that during a war many years ago residents of the village where it was found covered it with cement during a period of unrest so that if they were over run the enemy wouldn't realize how valuable it was.
  
That worked but apparently everyone who knew about it must have been killed because it wasn't until a few hundred years later, when one of the Kings was moving it to another location, that it was dropped because it was so heavy and some of the concrete broke off. To reveal the amazing golden Buddha.
  
It was quite beautiful and the temples around it very impressive.

Drew and Jo with Reclining Buddha.

Mary with lots of Buddhas.

      
The second temple was the one with the large and famous Reclining Buddha. It is an amazing piece of work over 47 meters long, not solid gold, but covered in gold.
  
I believe the temple and the grounds around it were the Kings personal religious area, with many small temples and Pagodas in the area.
  

Our third stop was the Jim Thompson estate. He was an American that fell in love with Thailand during the Second World War and moved there shortly afterwards.
  
He was an architect and designer and fell in love with the Thai silk makers who were mostly a cottage industry and not well organized or successful.
  
He made them both and really broke through when he supplied most of the costumes and silk for he movie The King And I with Yul Brenner.
  
He did very well creating his own brand and trading company and used the profits to buy up a lot of the antiques in the area and built an amazing house complex, that was mostly antique houses he bought in other areas and moved to Bangkok that he filled with his antique treasures.
  
He disappeared without a trace while he was on holidays in Kuala Lumpur and as he had no immediate family, his house, company and antiques became part of a foundation.
  
You see Jim Thompson stores in most the malls and shopping areas. Very expensive!
  
It was a fun visit.
   

     
Our guide suggested that we come back to the hotel by sky train so he can show us how to take the free boat from our hotel to the night market. Taking the sky train was an interesting experience travelling with the locals. It is a very modern system not unlike the one in Vancouver.

      
After we got back to the hotel while others went to rest in their rooms, Ron and I went out to shop at the antique stores in an adjacent mall.
  
Ron has become quite the shopper.
  
We looked at a number of beautiful things but didn't buy anything.

Next day we got up early to go see the floating market and what is called the train market which we went to first.
  
The train market is right in the middle of a small town, about 45 Minutes from Bangkok and is a market right on the train tracks.
  
When the train comes through all the vendors pull there awnings and tables back from the rails to let the train pass.
  
The train was moving quite slowly but it was fun to see.

Lots of coconut, banana, and Papaya trees seemingly everywhere. You can see how “the living is easy here" which is a good thing as with the heat and humidity you need lots of rest and laying about time. Railroad track market.

Interestingly where the

 sales people up to now have not been aggressive and seemed to be happy to just let us walk away, these ones were not.

The Elephant place was interesting.
  
On the plane in I read an article about a woman who preaches against Elephant rides and performing, maintaining that it is unnatural and cruel, that the wooden platforms are hard on their backs and that we should just take care of them and be their friends, not their "Ring Masters" so to speak.
  
Also the article was really down on the use of Bull Hooks and chains on the Elephants.
  
That said, we went to a place where there were Bull Hooks and chains, and the Elephants played soccer, threw darts at balloons, drew paintings, and gave rides with wooden platforms on their backs.
  
There were a lot of families there and everyone seemed to have a great time, even the Elephants&#8230; 
hopefully.
  
We had our pictures taken while we fed them bananas and sugar cane, watched them bathe in the river and their paintings were surprisingly good.
  
A good time was had by all&#8230; hopefully

      
From there we went to a village area where there were a number of different native tribes, the most noticeable of which is the Long Necked people.
  
Though here were 4 other tribes too, including the &#8216;long ear lobe' tribe.
  
I think we have a few of them living in Vancouver!
  
The &#8216;long neckers' put rings around their necks of the girls who are born on Thursday or Friday to lengthen their necks.
  
I suppose they feel it makes them more beautiful.
  
Kind of reminds me of the binding of the feet that they used to do in China.
  
They seemed happy and hopefully there is no major damage to them physically.
  
For the convenience of the tourists their villages were located adjacent to each other in some rice fields in a rather beautiful setting.
  
Most of the women were weaving and of course, as always there were lots of shopping and photo opportunities.

Day two in Chiang Mai.

     
Nan, the Cahoon's Thai exchange student and her mother whose name is pronounced "air" picked us up at 8:30 AM in a new Toyota van they rented for the day.
  
Air is a beautiful lady who worked for the Bernina sewing machine company here in Thailand and her husband is head of the tourist police here in Chiang Mai.
  
She has a wonderful relationship with her daughters, Nan and Ann, whom the Cahoon's also know and who coincidentally is working at the hotel we are staying at, in the Spa department.

     
We started by driving quite a ways up a mountain to a Buddhist temple that was quite amazing.
  
There were a lot of visitors from devout Buddhists to tourists like us.
  
We took a vernicular type elevator from the car park to the temple. Unfortunately it was quite foggy so we could not see the city.
  
It was really interesting to see the various expressions of faith exhibited at the temple.
  
Lots of gold spires and monks.

      
We then went up further up the mountain to the King's palace.
  
A secondary palace that they go to when it gets too hot in Bangkok.
  
Chiang Mai is higher up and definitely cooler. The day was actually quite pleasant weather wise until we came back down the mountain to the heat and humidity.
  
At the palace we we were only able to visit the gardens but they were beautiful, though not currently in full bloom.

     
From there we came down the mountain and went to lunch at a fun organic restaurant started by 3 guys who grow all the food for their restaurants in their own green houses.
  
Wonderful salads!!
  
After lunch Air got us the opportunity to visit the Bernina factory where she worked until just recently.
  
It was really interesting, though we were unable to take any pictures.
 
 
You can imagine how complicated these modern sewing machines are, and they not only assemble them, they also manufacture most of the parts here.
  
Quite amazing!!
  
We were given an extensive tour by Martin, the head guy who is from Switzerland and obviously very proud of the company he has built here, and very fond of Air who was greeted very warmly at each part of the factory.
  
He told me that labour savings by having the factory here as opposed to Switzerland is a factor of 8.
  
They simply could not stay in business if they manufactured all their products in Europe.
  
They still have a small plant there but it sounds like eventually all manufacturing will be in Thailand.
  
It was very interesting.
  
At one point they were thinking of moving to
  
Viet Nam, that would have saved even more on the labour, but decided it was best to expand the Thailand plant.

      
We then came back to the hotel where we surprised Nan's sister Ann.
  
Both such lovely girls who get along so well with each other and their mother.
  
It was decided that after such a great lunch we really didn't need dinner Air had planned.
  
With the dinner there was to be a cultural presentation but because of the death of the King that presentation was cancelled, so we decided to part company until our last day together where amongst other things we were going to their home for dinner prepared by the police captain!

     
Ron and I then went for a swim before taking the free shuttle to the night market for one last bit of shopping and a massage.
  
You can get a massage here almost anywhere.
  
Nice end to another interesting day in Chiang Mai.
