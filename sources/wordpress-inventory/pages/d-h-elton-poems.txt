D. H. Elton Poems
		

		

		
 

Grandpa wrote a lot of great poems, in fact literally hundreds of them.  He would write poems for important events in the lives of his wife and children.  He would write them for his Kiwanis Club, his Liberal Party, his Church, his City, all of which he loved passionately. He would write a poem for a friend or neighbour in need or an event he was asked to speak at.   In short, Grandpa didn't need much of an excuse to write a poem; he was very prolific and could write a poem if not every day of his life, at least every week.  (And he lived a long time!)  Needless to say we do not have a full collection of his poetic writings here, but a smattering of what has become some of the more popular ones.  

Many of them became classics that have been memorized and passed on.  We now realize a few poems we have attributed to him were probably not written by him.  I expect that happened because he not only wrote poetry but loved and collected it from others, copying them down in his own hand.  Remember, the type writer was not invented, or at least in common use, until much later in his life.  After his death his descendants, going through his considerable journals and other writings, seeing those poems mixed in with his own, attributed them and published them as his.  I believe that is the case with Guy In the Glass.  

So, the below congregation of poems were in large part written by David Horton Elton and whether by him or not, contain wonderful thoughts he felt were worthy of passing on.  As one of his grandsons, who has memorized, recited and enjoyed them over these  many years, I thank him.  He was indeed a wonderful poet, and A Regular First Classed Guy!
