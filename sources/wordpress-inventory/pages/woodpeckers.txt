The Woodpecker
								

				

				

				

									
Grandpa owned a cottage at Waterton Lake which was shingled on the sides and often visited by woodpeckers which riddled the shingles from top to bottom in search of flies and bugs.
								

				

				

				

							

			

		

						

				

				

				

									

T

he Woodpecker knows what his beak’s for and so 
He cannot be fooled for a minute,
He isn’t attracted by silver and gold,
To try and find out what is in it.

He stays with the job nature fitted him for, 
And avoids the concrete and stone.

You won’t catch him pecking at iron or steel, 
He leaves them politely alone.

He does his work well, as the poor flies can tell, 
He knows where they go for the winter,

With him it’s a lark, to cling to the bark, 
And hammer it down to a splinter.

He can hang upside down, on the square or the round — 
‘Twon’t make any difference, you see,

His feet made for hanging, his beak made for banging 
At shingles or bark on the tree.

Let’s all take a lesson, quit quizzin’ and guessin’ 
‘Bout things don’t concern us at all —

Quit foolin’ and fussin’ and messin’ and mussin’,

But go to the bat and play ball.

Like the woodpecker — know what to do and just so

Make a hit and get on for a run, 

Play the right alley, and score up a tally,

For there’s plenty of work to be done.

If only we’d stick, through thin and through thick, 
At the work we’re best fitted to do,

We could all get ahead, and earn daily bread, 
And journey successfully through.

But we foozle and fret, and struggle and sweat, 
And sniffle, and sizzle, and sob,

Just because we can’t be like the bird in the tree, 
THE WOODPECKER WHO STICKS TO HIS JOB! 

•D.H. Elton•
