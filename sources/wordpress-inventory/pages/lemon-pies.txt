Lemon Pies!
								

				

				

				

									
Tonight I’m going to be a Po-et
And rhyme my words before you know it
And if perchance my looks don’t show it!
Just hold your breath and watch me go-it.

And as I pen my thoughts tonight
Upon this sheet of paper white,
My mind reverts to days gone by,
Of Custards sweet and Lemon Pie!

I’ll tell you Dora, ‘tis the truth,
If maids would win the handsome youth
They’d better your example try,
And feed the lads on Lemon Pie!

Now you may say, “It’s all a bluff,
Men do not care for spicy stuff,
But I’ll tell you, as one who knows
From whence the love of mankind flows.

The language shall be plain and terse
Found in the last lines of this verse,
The spring wherein his deep love lies,
Is only reached by Lemon Pies!

Then! If the spark of love you’d fan
Within the bosom of a man!
That it may glow and richly burn
And to your own sweet heart return!

Just stir it up with spices fine,
And you will conquer every time,
There’ll be no sorrows, sobs or sighs
When he beholds your Lemon Pies.

But come! Enough of Lemon Pie,
So let us pass the subject by,
And, once for all, just hear me say,
Your Lemon Pies will win the day.

Yes! Let us seek a better clime,
And speak of things that’re more sublime,
We’re meeting with success in Rome
And all remember you at home!

Though you are many miles away
I to the Lord will every pray
That He may bless you every day,
And lead you in the Narrow Way!

May peace, success and happiness
Crown all your days with raptured bliss,
While I — Defender of the Faith,
Remain as ever — William H.

— Written for Mr. William H. Collins of Rome,

Georgia, to his lady love, Miss Dora Philemon at

Battle Creek, Michigan, while Elder Stokes and I

were laboring in that city — Rome. 1899
