D.H. Elton Poems
								

				

				

				

									
Our Grandpa David Horton Elton led an amazing life covering 86 years.  Born in England in 1870, one of 12 children he lost his father when he was 3 years old, emigrated to Utah with his mother when he was 13.  He served a mission for his church in the Southern States at 21, where he met the girl who would later become his wife.  He was called to go to Canada by Lorenzo Snow, the then president of his church, he started newspapers got a law degree through correspondence, was an amazing writer, successful lawyer, justice of the peace and politician.  Notably he became a revered and long serving mayor of Lethbridge, Alberta.  He had 5 remarkable children and an amazing life all the way around.

Grandpa’s love for poetry came from his mother, Isabella Amelia Horton, who unfortunately only left a very few poems for her posterity.  He never lost his zeal for poetry, which he carried on throughout his life, leaving a wonderful written legacy for friends and family.  He would write poems for important events in the lives of his wife and children.  He would write them for his Kiwanis Club, his Liberal Party, his Church, Country and City.  He would write a poem for a family member, a friend or neighbor in need, or for an event he was asked to speak at.  In short, Grandpa didn’t need much of an excuse to write a poem; he was very prolific and could write a poem if not every day of his life, at least every week.  (And he lived a long time!)  Needless to say, we do not have a full collection of his poetic writings here, but a smattering of some of the more popular ones.  (If you know of additional poems you feel should be added please send them to me.)

Many became family classics memorized and passed on down through the years.  He was a great collector of poems from others of his era, particularly Edgar A. Guest, and we now realize a few poems we initially attributed to him were probably not written by him.  We expect that happened because he not only wrote poetry but loved and collected it from others, copying them down in his own hand.  Remember, even the typewriter was not in common use, until later in his life.  I believe we found that to be the case with “The Guy In the Glass” which had become a family classic memorized and recited by many family members.

So, the below congregation of poems were mostly written by David Horton Elton and whether by him or not, contain wonderful thoughts he felt were worthy of passing on.  As one of his grandsons, who has memorized, recited and enjoyed them over these many years, I thank him.  He was indeed a wonderful poet, and “A Regular First Class Guy”!
