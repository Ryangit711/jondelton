The 

Spider
								

				

				

				

									
A spider was born one early spring morn,
And lived in the top of an old country barn.
He spun his web high, and looked out on the sky,
And anxiously waited for flies passing by.
But his patience gave out, for no flies came about,
So he said, “Down below I’ll just go and scout,
For I hear down below that flies to and fro,
Are humming, and bumming their way as they go.”
So a single line spun he went down to the Humm,
Of his victims the flies, and his work he begun.
There were plenty of flies, and bugs there besides.
So our spider concluded he’d acted quite wise.
With another web spun, in the corner, he hung.
Grew old and waxed fat all the summer day long.

One day it is said, in circling his web,
He discovered that single thread over his head,
And remarked, “What the deuce, there’s really no use.
For this bit of thread so I’ll cut myself loose.”
Quite forgetting that day he came down that way
From the loft where his first web was anchored to stay
So without any pause, and discarding all laws,
He severed the thread with his scissor- like claws;
“’Twas useless”, he said, “By this thread to hang”
IPSO FACTO–the whole thing collapsed with a bang:
For all, right along, by this thread was made strong,
From the beam in the loft, where his first web was hung.

And the moral friend mine is the ‘ere keep in line,
With the thread overhead –so exceedingly fine.

For the thread, overhead connects with God’s love,
Binding mortals, secure to the great throne above.
It’s Liberty, Friendship, Justice and Right,
Equality, Freedom, Mercy and Light.
It’s Virtue, and Love in this mortal probation,
It’s Wisdom and Truth to a World generation.
Its Confidence, Trust, in ourselves, and each other,
It’s Prayer and it’s Faith, binding brother to brother;
It’s Service-unselfish -willing-complete,
To smooth out the path, and guide weary feet.
It brightens the Day, and points out the Way,
For those who may otherwise wander and stray.
Then, let us not sever this fine, vital thread,
But be ever sustained by the line over head.
