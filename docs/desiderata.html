My Desiderata

Desiderata — Latin for “Desired Things”

Introduction

My Desiderata came out of a business trip I made to Calgary many years ago, when I stayed with my cousin Pamela Lehto in her lovely town house. On the wall of her guest bathroom was a plaque with Desiderata by Max Ehrmann.

I remembered it from back in 1971, when a spoken word version by Les Crane was played on the radio and made it to #8 on the Billboard chart of the day, #4 in Canada. I loved the thoughts it contained, committed it to memory, and over the years, as I have recited it to myself, I found a few bits I didn’t like so much that I left out, and added a number of my own thoughts — making it “Our” Desiderata.

It has become my mantra, so to speak, and I recite it to myself pretty much every day, most often during my morning shower. It’s still evolving as I add and change it to reflect my feelings about what “things are worth desiring” in this life. I share it with you below.

My Desiderata

by Max Ehrmann and Jon Elton

Go calmly amid the noise and haste and remember what peace may be found in silence. As far as possible, without surrender, be on good terms with all persons. Speak your truth quietly and clearly, and listen to others, even those who can do nothing to you, or for you, for they too have their story, and you just never know. If you compare yourself to others, you may become vain or bitter, for always there will be those greater and lesser than yourself. Enjoy your achievements as well as your plans. Keep interested in your own career, however humble, it’s a real possession in the changing fortunes of time. Exercise caution in your business affairs, for the world is full of trickery, but let that not blind you to the virtue there is, for many seek after high ideals and everywhere, life is full of heroism. Be yourself, especially do not feign affection or be cynical about love, for in the face of all aridity and disenchantment it is as perennial as the grass. Take kindly the counsel of the years, gracefully surrendering the things of youth. Nurture strength of spirit that it may shield you from sudden misfortune, but do not distress yourself with dark imaginings. Many fears are born from fatigue, misunderstanding and loneliness. And when facing hardships move through them quickly, for the longer we dwell on our misfortunes, the greater is their power to do us damage. Beyond a wholesome discipline, be gentle with yourself, for you are a child of the universe, no less than the trees and the stars, you have a right to be here. And whether or not it is clear to you, no doubt the universe is unfolding as it should. Therefore be at peace with God, whatever you conceive Him to be. And whatever your fears or aspirations in this busy confused world, keep peace in your soul, for all its shame, drudgery and broken dreams, it is still a beautiful world. Be cheerful, be a good friend, be forgiving, for we all need forgiveness and remember it is usually more important to the giver than to the receiver. Be grateful and show it. Be generous to a fault. Have faith, it’s what gives us hope! Don’t take yourself too seriously, And strive to be happy, it is a choice.
